## Items (priority order)

### 1. [Priority 7] Agencies Remove Additional References to Reputation Risk in 15 Interagency Documents — Federal Reserve / OCC / FDIC, Federal

- **Type**: Manual/Guidance Update
- **Date**: 2026-06-02
- **Summary**: The Federal Reserve Board, OCC, and FDIC jointly reissued 15 interagency supervisory guidance documents with all references to "reputation risk" removed. The updated documents span asset securitization, subprime lending, bank-owned life insurance (BOLI), customer identification program FAQs, home equity credit risk, remote deposit capture, counterparty credit risk, cyber-incident statements (ATM/card-authorization attacks, DDoS, cyber insurance, cyber extortion), operational resilience, elder financial exploitation, and sales of 100% loan participations. The action is a follow-on to the agencies' April 2026 final rule that eliminated reputation risk as a supervisory category and is intended to prevent regulators from using reputational considerations to pressure banks away from legal customers or industries. Effective immediately.
- **Link**: <https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/bcreg20260602a.htm>
- **Also see**: OCC Bulletin 2026-23 (<https://www.occ.gov/news-issuances/bulletins/2026/bulletin-2026-23.html>); FDIC (<https://www.fdic.gov/news/press-releases/2026/agencies-remove-additional-references-reputation-risk>)

### 2. [Priority 6] SEC Proposes Rescission of Climate-Related Disclosure Rules — SEC, Federal

- **Type**: Proposed Rule
- **Date**: 2026-05-29
- **Summary**: The Securities and Exchange Commission voted to propose the full rescission of the climate-related disclosure rules adopted in March 2024 (Release No. 33-11275). The proposal argues the rules exceed the SEC's statutory authority under the Securities Act and Exchange Act and conflict with a registrant-specific, materiality-based approach to disclosure. Affected entities include all public companies filing registration statements and annual reports — prominently including bank holding companies and their subsidiaries. The proposal would rescind all climate-risk, Scope 1/2/3 greenhouse-gas, and scenario-analysis disclosure requirements. A 60-day public comment period opens upon Federal Register publication.
- **Link**: <https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2026-49-sec-proposes-rescission-climate-related-disclosure-rules>

### 3. [Priority 5] FHA Request for Information: Single Family Minimum Property Requirements (MPR) — HUD / FHA, Federal

- **Type**: Request for Information (pre-rulemaking)
- **Date**: 2026-05-29
- **Summary**: HUD's Federal Housing Administration published a Request for Information (Docket No. FR-6609-N-01) in the Federal Register seeking public feedback on how it might modernize its Minimum Property Requirements for single-family homes insured under FHA programs. FHA states it is contemplating a reassessment of MPR policies to balance collateral-risk management with reducing barriers to homeownership; the RFI asks about specific MPR provisions and general modernization approaches. FHA-approved lenders and institutions originating FHA-insured mortgage loans should review and consider submitting comments. Comment deadline: June 29, 2026.
- **Link**: <https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/05/29/2026-10766/request-for-information-regarding-single-family-minimum-property-requirements-mpr>

### 4. [Priority 4] FDIC Publishes April 2026 Enforcement Actions — FDIC, Federal

- **Type**: Enforcement
- **Date**: 2026-05-29
- **Summary**: The FDIC released its monthly batch of April 2026 enforcement actions. Notable items include two orders for violations of the Flood Disaster Protection Act of 1973 — one for failure to timely notify a borrower about flood insurance availability on collateral located in a Special Flood Hazard Area, and another for failure to force-place flood insurance in 20 loan cases. A third action is an informal agreement with a bank for deficiencies in its AML/CFT program, including weaknesses in risk assessment, Customer Due Diligence (CDD), Suspicious Activity Reporting (SAR), and Currency Transaction Reporting (CTR) procedures, and the absence of a qualified AML officer and formal AML training program.
- **Link**: <https://www.fdic.gov/news/press-releases/2026/fdic-publishes-april-enforcement-actions>

### 5. [Priority 3] Prudential Bank Regulators Testify Before House Financial Services Committee — Federal Reserve / OCC / FDIC / NCUA, Federal

- **Type**: News-Announcement
- **Date**: 2026-06-04
- **Summary**: Federal Reserve Vice Chair for Supervision Michelle Bowman, Comptroller Jonathan Gould, FDIC Chairman Travis Hill, and Acting NCUA Chairman Kyle Hauptman testified at a House Financial Services Committee oversight hearing titled "Oversight of Prudential Regulators." The regulators collectively outlined their deregulatory agendas, including right-sizing capital requirements, refocusing examination frameworks on material financial risk rather than procedural shortcomings, and building supervisory frameworks for stablecoins and AI. Bowman noted that frontier AI advances have "dramatically accelerated" the identification of cyber vulnerabilities across critical infrastructure including the banking system. No binding regulatory action resulted from the hearing.
- **Link**: <https://www.fdic.gov/news/speeches/2026/oversight-prudential-regulators>
