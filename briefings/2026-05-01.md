## Items (priority order)

### 1. [Priority 9] OCC Issues Interim Final Rule on National Bank Non-Interest Charges and Fees and Interim Final Order Preempting the Illinois IFPA — OCC, Federal
- **Type**: Interim Final Rule + Interim Final Order
- **Date**: 2026-04-29 (Federal Register publication; effective 2026-06-30)
- **Summary**: The OCC published two related interim final actions in the April 29 Federal Register. The **Interim Final Rule** (12 CFR Part 7) clarifies that national banks may charge non-interest fees and charges — including interchange fees on payment-card transactions — regardless of whether those fees are set by the bank or a third party, restoring longstanding federal preemption of conflicting state fee restrictions. The **Interim Final Order** directly preempts Illinois's Interchange Fee Prohibition Act (IFPA) for national banks and federal savings associations, concluding that federal law governs and that institutions are neither subject to nor required to comply with the IFPA. Both actions take effect June 30, 2026 — one day before the IFPA's scheduled July 1, 2026 effective date. National banks and FSAs with Illinois operations or payment-card programs should review the rule text and coordinate with counsel; comment on the interim final rule is still open.
- **Link**: https://www.occ.treas.gov/news-issuances/news-releases/2026/nr-occ-2026-32.html
  - Federal Register (Non-Interest Charges IFR): https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/04/29/2026-08328/national-bank-non-interest-charges-and-fees
  - Federal Register (IFPA Preemption Order): https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/04/29/2026-08341/order-preempting-the-illinois-interchange-fee-prohibition-act

### 2. [Priority 7] NACHA Approves Same Day ACH Per-Payment Limit Increase to $10 Million — NACHA, Federal
- **Type**: Rule (NACHA operating rule, approved by membership)
- **Date**: 2026-04-27 (announced at Smarter Faster Payments 2026 conference, San Diego; effective 2027-09-17)
- **Summary**: NACHA's membership voted to raise the Same Day ACH per-transaction dollar limit from $1 million to $10 million. The increase takes effect September 17, 2027, giving all ACH network participants approximately 17 months to update systems, risk models, and operational processes. The new limit is the third increase in Same Day ACH history ($25K → $100K in 2020; $100K → $1M in March 2022; $1M → $10M now). NACHA expects the higher cap to expand Same Day ACH use for large invoice and tax payments, payroll funding, insurance claim payments, merchant settlement, and cash concentration. ODFIs, RDFIs, and third-party senders should assess credit-risk limits, fraud monitoring thresholds, and intraday liquidity needs well ahead of the September 2027 date.
- **Link**: https://www.nacha.org/news/same-day-ach-payment-limit-increase-10-million

### 3. [Priority 6] OCC Proposes to Streamline Regulations on Public Welfare Investments, Open Market CLOs, and FSA Nondiscrimination Requirements — OCC, Federal
- **Type**: Proposed Rule
- **Date**: 2026-04-24 (OCC bulletin); 2026-04-27 (Federal Register publication)
- **Summary**: OCC Bulletin 2026-16 proposes to rescind or amend three sets of regulations under 12 CFR Parts 24, 43, and 128, consistent with Executive Order 14219 on deregulation. (1) **Part 24 (Public Welfare Investments)**: Would remove references to minority- and women-owned entities from PWI eligibility criteria. (2) **Part 43 (CLO Risk Retention)**: Would rescind the lead-arranger alternative compliance option for open-market collateralized loan obligations, tightening alignment with the interagency risk-retention rule. (3) **Part 128 (FSA Nondiscrimination)**: Would remove duplicative nondiscrimination requirements for federal savings associations already governed by other statutes. National banks with active PWI programs or CLO origination/retention programs should evaluate the impact. **Comment deadline: May 27, 2026.**
- **Link**: https://www.occ.gov/news-issuances/bulletins/2026/bulletin-2026-16.html
  - Federal Register: https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/04/27/2026-08143/streamlining-regulations-concerning-public-welfare-investments-open-market-collateralized-loan

### 4. [Priority 5] Federal Reserve FOMC: Policy Rate Held at 3½–3¾ Percent — Federal Reserve, Federal
- **Type**: News-Announcement
- **Date**: 2026-04-29 (effective 2026-04-30)
- **Summary**: The FOMC voted unanimously on April 29, 2026 to maintain the target range for the federal funds rate at 3½ to 3¾ percent. The Committee cited ongoing uncertainty about the economic outlook as justification for holding rates steady. The decision directly affects bank funding costs, deposit pricing, loan re-pricing timelines, and net interest margin projections. Banks should update their interest-rate risk models and ALM assumptions to reflect the continued pause.
- **Link**: https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260429a.htm
