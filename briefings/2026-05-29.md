## Items (priority order)

### 1. [Priority 8] Executive Order: Restoring Integrity to America's Financial System — White House / Treasury, Federal
- **Type**: Executive Order (Presidential Action)
- **Date**: 2026-05-19 (signed); 2026-05-22 (Federal Register publication)
- **Summary**: Directs Treasury to issue a formal BSA advisory identifying red flags for immigration-related financial activity (payroll tax evasion, ITIN misuse, structuring, labor trafficking). Within 60 days, CFPB must consider clarifying that potential deportation is an ability-to-repay risk factor for non-work-authorized borrowers. Within 90 days, Treasury must propose amended CDD/KYC rules to strengthen customer identity verification. Within 180 days, Treasury and functional regulators must consider tightening CIP requirements regarding foreign consular IDs. All banks, credit unions, and other regulated entities will face potential BSA program amendments.
- **Link**: https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/05/22/2026-10400/restoring-integrity-to-americas-financial-system

### 2. [Priority 7] Executive Order: Integrating Financial Technology Innovation into Regulatory Frameworks — White House / Multiple Agencies, Federal
- **Type**: Executive Order (Presidential Action)
- **Date**: 2026-05-19 (signed); 2026-05-22 (Federal Register publication)
- **Summary**: Directs each federal financial regulator (OCC, FDIC, NCUA, Fed, CFPB, SEC, CFTC) to complete a 90-day review of regulations, guidance, and application processes that impede fintech firms from partnering with federally regulated institutions or obtaining bank/credit-union charters. The Federal Reserve is separately directed to evaluate within 120 days whether uninsured depositories and non-bank financial companies (including digital asset firms) should obtain direct access to Federal Reserve Bank payment accounts and services. Banks should anticipate incoming guidance changes and potential charter/partnership rule revisions.
- **Link**: https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/05/22/2026-10399/integrating-financial-technology-innovation-into-regulatory-frameworks

### 3. [Priority 7] FDIC NPRM: BSA/Sanctions Compliance Standards for FDIC-Supervised Permitted Payment Stablecoin Issuers — FDIC, Federal
- **Type**: Proposed Rule
- **Date**: 2026-05-22
- **Summary**: The FDIC Board approved a notice of proposed rulemaking that would require FDIC-supervised permitted payment stablecoin issuers (PPSIs) to establish BSA/AML/CFT programs and comply with OFAC sanctions requirements consistent with FinCEN and OFAC regulations. This is the third GENIUS Act rulemaking at the FDIC (following the December 2025 application-procedures rule and the April 2026 prudential-standards rule). Banks exploring stablecoin issuance should track the comment period. Comment period dates to be confirmed in the Federal Register notice.
- **Link**: https://www.fdic.gov/news/press-releases/2026/fdic-board-approves-proposal-address-bank-secrecy-act-and-sanctions

### 4. [Priority 6] Federal Reserve NPRM: Regulation A Amendments — Discount Window Restrictions for Payment Account Holders — Federal Reserve, Federal
- **Type**: Proposed Rule
- **Date**: 2026-05-26 (Federal Register publication; 2026-10376)
- **Summary**: The Federal Reserve Board proposed amendments to Regulation A (Extensions of Credit by Federal Reserve Banks) that would prohibit Reserve Banks from extending discount window credit to Payment Account holders — entities that would hold a new type of Fed master account under the companion Payment Account proposal (previously announced May 20, 2026). The proposal would explicitly ring-fence discount window access away from these non-bank entrants. Comment period closes July 27, 2026.
- **Link**: https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/05/26/2026-10376/regulation-a-extensions-of-credit-by-federal-reserve-banks

### 5. [Priority 5] Fed and FDIC Publish Resolution Plan Feedback Letters for 8 GSIBs and 56 Foreign Banking Organizations — Federal Reserve / FDIC, Federal
- **Type**: Guidance / Exam Update
- **Date**: 2026-05-22
- **Summary**: The Federal Reserve and FDIC jointly published feedback letters on the July 2025 resolution plan submissions from the eight U.S. global systemically important banks (JPMorgan Chase, Bank of America, Citigroup, Goldman Sachs, Wells Fargo, Morgan Stanley, BNY, and State Street) and 56 foreign banking organizations. The agencies found no shortcomings or deficiencies in any submission and confirmed that derivatives-related weaknesses previously identified in 2023 plans for Bank of America, Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan Chase, and Citigroup have been satisfactorily remediated. OCC Comptroller Gould abstained from the FDIC vote, citing unresolved structural concerns with the resolution planning regime.
- **Link**: https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/bcreg20260522a.htm

### 6. [Priority 3] OCC Comptroller Issues Statement on Abstention from FDIC Resolution Plan Vote — OCC, Federal
- **Type**: News-Announcement
- **Date**: 2026-05-22
- **Summary**: Comptroller Jonathan Gould issued a statement explaining his abstention from the FDIC's vote on GSIB resolution plan feedback, arguing the current resolution planning process has unresolved legal and conceptual deficiencies and provides limited supervisory value. He has previously advocated for significant reform of the process. The statement signals continued OCC skepticism about the resolution planning framework and could foreshadow rulemaking or legislative advocacy to modify Title I/II requirements.
- **Link**: https://www.occ.gov/news-issuances/news-releases/2026/nr-occ-2026-41.html

### 7. [Priority 3] Kevin Warsh Sworn in as Federal Reserve Chair — Federal Reserve, Federal
- **Type**: News-Announcement
- **Date**: 2026-05-22
- **Summary**: Kevin Warsh took the oath of office at the White House before Supreme Court Justice Clarence Thomas, becoming the 11th chair of the Federal Reserve. Warsh was confirmed by the Senate on May 12–13, 2026. He succeeds Jerome Powell, who remains on the Board as a governor. At the swearing-in, Warsh stated he would lead a "reform-oriented Federal Reserve." His priorities are expected to include regulatory burden reduction, capital rule modernization, and reassessment of the Fed's supervisory posture — themes consistent with Vice Chair Bowman's supervisory operating principles update from April 2026.
- **Link**: https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/other20260522a.htm
