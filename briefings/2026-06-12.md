# Weekly Regulatory Change Briefing — Week of 2026-06-05 to 2026-06-11

_Run date: 2026-06-12. 9 items captured across 6 federal sources and 2 states (California, Oregon)._

## Top of the week

- **FDTA Joint Data Standards Final Rule (June 11):** All nine federal financial regulators jointly finalized machine-readable data standards required by the Financial Data Transparency Act of 2022, effective October 1, 2026. Every institution that reports to any participating agency faces new formatting and identifier requirements. [Link](https://www.occ.gov/news-issuances/bulletins/2026/bulletin-2026-25.html)
- **NCUA Interim Final Rule — FCU Fee Preemption (June 8):** NCUA issued an interim final rule asserting exclusive federal authority over FCU non-interest charges and fees (including interchange), preempting Illinois' IFPA and any similar state restrictions. Effective June 30, 2026. [Link](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/06/09/2026-11559/preemption-federal-credit-union-non-interest-charges-and-fees)
- **Oregon DIDMCA Opt-Out Effective (June 5):** Oregon's opt-out of federal interest-rate preemption under DIDMCA became law, capping out-of-state FDIC-insured state-chartered banks at Oregon's 36% APR limit for consumer loans ≤ $50,000. Immediate compliance review required for interstate consumer lenders. [Link](https://oregonbusinessreport.com/2026/06/new-law-protects-consumers-from-predatory-high-interest-loans/)

## Items (priority order)

### 1. [Priority 7] Financial Data Transparency Act: Joint Data Standards Final Rule — Multiple Agencies, Federal

- **Type**: Final Rule
- **Date**: 2026-06-11
- **Summary**: Nine federal financial regulators — the Federal Reserve, FDIC, OCC, CFPB, SEC, FHFA, NCUA, CFTC, and Treasury — jointly published a final rule implementing mandatory data standards under the Financial Data Transparency Act of 2022. The rule requires all institutions submitting regulatory data to those agencies to use common identifiers: ISO 17442 Legal Entity Identifier (LEI) for entities, ISO 4914 Unique Product Identifier for swaps, ISO 8601 for dates, and machine-readable schema formats. All data collections subject to the rule must comply by the effective date of October 1, 2026. This is a cross-cutting compliance project requiring coordination across reporting, IT, and data-governance functions.
- **Link**: https://www.occ.gov/news-issuances/bulletins/2026/bulletin-2026-25.html

### 2. [Priority 7] NCUA Interim Final Rule: Federal Credit Union Non-Interest Charges and Fees — NCUA, Federal

- **Type**: Interim Final Rule
- **Date**: 2026-06-08
- **Summary**: The NCUA published an interim final rule clarifying that federal credit unions' authority under the Federal Credit Union Act to charge non-interest fees — including interchange fees on credit and debit card operations — is exclusively governed by federal law and preempts any state restriction, including Illinois' Interchange Fee Prohibition Act. The rule expressly covers interchange fees set by or in consultation with third parties such as card networks. This mirrors the OCC's earlier interim final rule for national banks. Effective June 30, 2026; comments accepted through July 9, 2026.
- **Link**: https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/06/09/2026-11559/preemption-federal-credit-union-non-interest-charges-and-fees

### 3. [Priority 7] Oregon HB 4116: DIDMCA Opt-Out — Consumer Loan Interest Rate Cap Now Effective — Oregon Legislature, Oregon

- **Type**: Law
- **Date**: 2026-06-05
- **Summary**: Oregon's election to opt out of DIDMCA Section 521 federal preemption became effective June 5, 2026 (signed by Governor Kotek on April 7, 2026). The law bars out-of-state FDIC-insured state-chartered banks from charging more than Oregon's 36% APR cap on consumer finance loans of $50,000 or less made to Oregon borrowers, closing the home-state rate-export pathway used by high-rate internet lenders. Oregon joins Colorado, Minnesota, and a small number of other states in exercising this opt-out right. Interstate consumer lenders operating in Oregon must audit their product offerings and rate schedules for immediate compliance.
- **Link**: https://oregonbusinessreport.com/2026/06/new-law-protects-consumers-from-predatory-high-interest-loans/

### 4. [Priority 6] CFPB Statement: Ability to Repay and Immigration Status — CFPB, Federal

- **Type**: Guidance
- **Date**: 2026-06-08
- **Summary**: Consistent with Executive Order 14406 ("Restoring Integrity to America's Financial System"), the CFPB published a statement in the Federal Register reminding creditors that the ability-to-repay (ATR) requirements under TILA and Regulation Z may require consideration of a consumer's immigration status where that status could affect the consumer's ability to remain employed and generate income in the United States. The CFPB states this is particularly relevant for mortgage originations and certain open-end credit products. The statement is guidance only (not a rule with force of law) but creates compliance risk for lenders who do not document their ATR methodology with respect to immigration-status information that is in their possession.
- **Link**: https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/06/08/2026-11447/statement-on-ability-to-repay-and-immigration-status

### 5. [Priority 5] FinCEN Advisory FIN-2026-A002: Illicit Activity Linked to Non-Work-Authorized Workers — FinCEN / FDIC / OCC / NCUA, Federal

- **Type**: Guidance
- **Date**: 2026-06-05
- **Summary**: FinCEN, jointly with the FDIC, OCC, and NCUA, issued Advisory FIN-2026-A002 directing banks and credit unions to identify and file Suspicious Activity Reports (SARs) for transactions connected to payroll fraud schemes that employ non-work-authorized workers. Financial institutions reported over $2.5 billion in related suspicious activity in 2025. The advisory identifies 18 red flags (e.g., unregistered money services businesses providing off-books payroll, cash-intensive accounts with inconsistent business activity) and introduces the new SAR key term "FINANCIALINTEGRITY-2026-A002," which must appear in SAR field 2 when filing.
- **Link**: https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0523

### 6. [Priority 4] California DFPI: Second Modified Proposed Digital Financial Assets Law Regulations — CA DFPI, California

- **Type**: Proposed Rule
- **Date**: 2026-06-05
- **Summary**: The California Department of Financial Protection and Innovation issued a second modified version of its proposed regulations implementing the Digital Financial Assets Law (DFAL), after the Office of Administrative Law disapproved the prior text on May 19, 2026. Modifications clarify: (1) the requirement to complete Form MU1 as part of a DFAL license application; (2) reporting obligations for changes in executive officers, control persons, and responsible persons; and (3) factors the Commissioner will weigh in setting surety bond amounts. The DFAL license deadline remains July 1, 2026. Written comments are accepted from June 5 through June 22, 2026.
- **Link**: https://dfpi.ca.gov/rules-enforcement/laws-and-regulations/digital-financial-assets-law-regulations-opinions-releases/

- **Date**: 2026-06-08

### 9. [Priority 2] Governor Barr Speech: Concerns About Weakened Bank Supervision — Federal Reserve, Federal

- **Type**: News-Announcement
- **Date**: 2026-06-06
- **Summary**: Federal Reserve Governor Michael Barr delivered a speech at American University raising concerns that simultaneous reductions in capital requirements and lighter-touch supervision compound financial stability risks. Barr criticized recent changes to the CAMELS rating methodology for the 36 largest institutions as functionally "grade inflation," the curtailment of Matters Requiring Attention (MRA) issuances that leaves bank risk unaddressed, and the broad removal of reputation risk from the supervisory framework. The speech carries no direct regulatory or compliance effect but signals internal Federal Reserve Board tension over the current deregulatory direction.
- **Link**: https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/speech/barr20260606a.htm
